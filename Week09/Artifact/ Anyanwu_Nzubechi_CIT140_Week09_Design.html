01. Problem, Boundary & Approach Comparison R1

Problem Statement

An archive request can currently be handed to a runner before staff confirm its destination and reference code. Clerks copy paper slips into a shared spreadsheet register identified by row number with no required-field checks, and status updates are entered as free text without consistent staff attribution or timestamps. As a result, staff spend time locating requests or asking for corrections after dispatch, with no supplied measured error rate or service-time baseline.

Design Boundary

This design covers receiving a request at the service desk through delivery to the correct destination keeper, or return to the requester for clarification. Retrieving archive contents, payments, and replacing archive machinery are outside this design.

Comparison of Approaches A and B W09-S04

Evaluation Dimension Approach A: Improve Existing Register Approach B: Small Request Interface Chosen
Fit with Requirements AR-01 to AR-06 Retains the spreadsheet register and paper slip while adding explicit fields, staff checks, status rules, and clearer delivery acknowledgment; relies on repeated manual checks and staff coordination. Uses a guided form, review/status view, and correction state backed by a shared request record to support guided entry, role-scoped visibility, status/clarification history, and keyboard/plain-text clarity.
Practical Limits Service continues during transition and staff already know the spreadsheet and paper slip, but manual checks and coordination remain ongoing requirements. Service must continue during transition, the existing directory remains the reference source, contact details stay at the desk, and keyboard access/non-color clarity are required; implementation, training, and support are unapproved, and cost, time savings, vendor contracts, or automatic directory integration are not supplied.
One Key Tradeoff Familiar tools avoid unapproved software and training hurdles, but repeated manual checks leave more room for human oversight before handoff. Structured guidance, role scoping, and recorded history are clearer in the interface, but the approach requires unapproved implementation, training, and support before use.

Chosen Approach & Rationale

Selected Approach: Approach B Small Request Interface, represented as a Proposed design model/sequence rather than a working or approved system. It makes pre-dispatch checks, role-limited visibility, status transitions, and preserved clarification history explicit while keeping existing directory and desk-continuity limits clear.

02. Proposed System Design & Route Description R2

Design State: PROPOSED for review by Iona Venn and the Archive Council; not deployed or self-approved.

Short Route Description

Normal Route: A requester submits a slip at the service desk, the desk clerk records the request ID and six required fields and checks the directory CK-042 → East Records; CK-087 → West Records, sets status to Ready, and hands off a scoped delivery view without requester contact to the runner In Delivery, who delivers to the destination keeper for receipt acknowledgment Received.
Clarification & Re-Entry Route: If required fields are missing or the reference/destination fails directory validation, dispatch is blocked, status becomes Awaiting Clarification, the original issue is logged, the requester supplies corrections, the clerk appends the correction without erasing prior entries, and the request returns to the desk directory check before becoming Ready.

Step 1: Desk Intake & Pre-Dispatch Directory Check

Acting Role: Desk Clerk

Information In: Request slip details requester, contact, reference, destination, summary and supplied directory CK-042 → East Records; CK-087 → West Records.

Information Out / Recorded: Request ID plus all six fields ID, requester, contact, reference, destination, summary, validation result, and actor/timestamp log.

Decision & Next Step:

  • Complete & Valid Match: Set status to Ready with clerk and timestamp → hand off to Runner queue.
  • Missing Field or Unknown/Conflicting Reference: Block dispatch, set status to Awaiting Clarification with clerk and timestamp, record original issue → route to Step 2.

Step 2: Clarification & Append-Only Correction Rejoin

Acting Roles: Desk Clerk primary, Requester supplies correction

Information In: Clarification notice and requester's corrected details e.g., reference CK-042 and destination East Records.

Information Out / Recorded: Appended correction entry with actor and timestamp while preserving the original issue and prior values.

Decision & Next Step / Outcome: Re-enter Step 1 validation check. Once valid, status changes to Ready and rejoins the normal dispatch route to the Runner.

Step 3: Runner Delivery Handoff

Acting Role: Runner

Information In / Visible: Request ID, reference, destination, and delivery status requester contact details excluded.

Information Out / Recorded: Status updated to In Delivery with runner role/ID and timestamp; next responsible role and destination identified.

Decision & Next Step: Transport request to the named destination and hand off to Destination Keeper.

Step 4: Destination Keeper Receipt & Acknowledgment

Acting Role: Destination Keeper

Information In / Visible: Request ID, reference, destination, and delivery status.

Information Out / Recorded: Receipt acknowledgment with keeper role/ID and timestamp; status set to Received or routing/information issue flagged back to desk clerk.

Outcome: Acknowledged delivery completes the normal route within the design boundary.

03. Requirements Traceability, Data Quality & Access R3

Data Quality & Role Access Summary

Data Quality: Every request requires an ID and all six fields before dispatch, and the desk clerk checks the reference against the directory CK-042 → East Records; CK-087 → West Records before setting status to Ready. Missing or conflicting information enters Awaiting Clarification, and corrections are appended while preserving original entries and recording who changed status and when.
Role Access & Authority: Requesters supply requests/corrections and receive status/next actions; desk clerks record requests, run checks, and handle clarification; runners see request ID, reference, destination, and delivery status without requester contact details; destination keepers acknowledge receipt or flag routing/information problems; Iona Venn reviews the proposal and unresolved questions; the Archive Council authorizes any operational change; the service team implements approved changes; and the systems analyst proposes and explains without deploying or self-approving. This is an unapproved proposal with no measured speed or error-rate claims.

04. Walkthroughs, Revisions, Risks & Next Test R4

Walkthrough N01: Complete Request Q-241

Input: Q-241 | Requester: Tessa Rowe | Contact: desk mailbox T-14 | Reference: CK-042 | Destination: East Records | Summary: request a copy of the gate maintenance record.

  1. Step 1 Desk Clerk: Records Q-241 with all six fields and checks directory: CK-042 maps to East Records, matching the stated destination.
  2. Step 1 Decision → Ready: Validation passes; status is set to Ready with clerk ID and timestamp.
  3. Step 3 Runner: Views Q-241, CK-042, East Records, and delivery status desk mailbox T-14 excluded; status updates to In Delivery with runner ID and timestamp.
  4. Step 4 Destination Keeper: East Records keeper receives request and records acknowledgment; status updates to Received with keeper ID and timestamp.

Expected Outcome & Gap Check: Reaches East Records with full status/actor/time trace and no contact exposure to the runner. No missing owner or broken return path found on N01; no revision required for the normal path after checking every handoff and status change.

Walkthrough E01: Incomplete Request Before & After Correction Q-242

Initial Input: Q-242 | Requester: Daro Lin | Contact: desk mailbox D-08 | Reference: CK-099 | Destination: blank | Summary: request a copy of the gate maintenance record. Correction Supplied: Reference CK-042 and destination East Records.

  1. Before Correction — Step 1 Intake & Check: Desk clerk enters Q-242; directory check finds no entry for CK-099 and destination is blank.
  2. Before Correction — Blocked & Clarification: Dispatch is blocked; status becomes Awaiting Clarification with clerk ID and timestamp, recording original issue unknown reference CK-099 and blank destination and routing to Daro Lin via desk mailbox D-08.
  3. After Correction — Append History: Daro Lin supplies reference CK-042 and destination East Records; desk clerk appends the correction to Q-242 while keeping the original CK-099 and blank-destination record intact.
  4. After Correction — Re-Check & Dispatch: Desk clerk re-runs directory check CK-042 → East Records, updates status to Ready with actor and timestamp, and dispatches through Runner In Delivery to East Records Keeper Received.

Expected Outcome: Q-242 cannot be marked Ready while incomplete/unmatched, and the final record shows both the initial issue and the appended correction.

Gap Found, Revision, Assumption/Risk & Next Test

Gap Found & Revision: An initial draft could allow a clerk to overwrite CK-099 in place or skip re-checking the directory after receiving a correction. Revised Step 2 so corrections are appended without erasing the original issue and explicitly return to the Step 1 directory check before setting status to Ready.
Assumption / Risk: Assumes staff follow the workflow and record accurate actors/timestamps during transition; risk remains that unapproved implementation/training delays or manual paper handoffs during transition could cause inconsistent logging.
Next Test Needed: Conduct a structured walkthrough/tabletop test with Iona Venn and desk, runner, and keeper representatives using N01, E01, and a conflicting reference/destination case to verify status history, role views, and keyboard/plain-text clarity before any Archive Council decision.
